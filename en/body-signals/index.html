Body signals

How are you feeling?

Feeling good

I feel fine

What's happening

The hard start is behind you and your brain is running on fat. You won't feel a rush, and you won't feel unwell either. Your metabolism has settled down.

What to do

Keep up the minerals

You feel good, but keep topping up salt and the other minerals.

Be patient

You may not notice anything, but your body is still doing its work in the background.

Carry on as usual

This is a good time for everyday tasks, walks or focused work.

I feel great

What's happening

Around day 3 or 4, many people get a burst of mental and physical energy. Your body releases more growth hormone and adrenaline to keep you going.

What to do

Use the focus

Take on hard tasks, study, or spend time on a creative hobby.

Move a little

Light exercise can lift your mood further.

Remember it

Notice how this feels. Recalling it will help during the low points of your next fast.

Something's off

Hunger pangs

What's happening

Hunger doesn't build steadily. It comes in waves, and a wave usually passes on its own in 15-20 minutes.

What to do

Salt on your tongue

Let a few grains of sea salt dissolve on your tongue. It often stops the craving quickly.

Sparkling water

The bubbles fill your stomach, so you feel less hungry.

Wait 15 minutes

Keep busy for 15 minutes: read, tidy up, garden. The wave often passes before you notice.

Dizzy when standing up

What's happening

With low insulin your body sheds water and salt, and your blood pressure drops. Getting up quickly can then make you dizzy.

What to do

Salt water

Drink a little salty water. It usually steadies your circulation quickly.

Get up slowly

Sit up first, wait a few seconds, then stand.

Headache

What's happening

Usually it's low salt, or your body is still adjusting to the switch.

What to do

Salt water

A glass of water with a pinch of salt often helps within minutes.

Magnesium

Take some magnesium as well. It helps relax the muscles in your neck and head.

Tired or weak

What's happening

Your muscles and nerves need minerals (electrolytes) to work properly, and you may be running low.

What to do

Electrolytes

Check whether you've had enough salt, potassium and magnesium today. If not, top them up now.

Fresh air

A short walk helps your body burn fat, which can bring some energy back.

Feeling cold

What's happening

Your body is saving energy and sending blood to your vital organs, so your hands and feet may get cold.

What to do

Fasting broth

Hot water with salt, pepper, basil, turmeric, parsley or other herbs. It warms you up and gives you salt at the same time.

Move

Do 15 squats to get blood back into your hands and feet.

Muscle cramps

What's happening

Cramps almost always mean you're short on magnesium or potassium.

What to do

Potassium and salt

Drink water with salt and potassium to help your muscles relax.

Magnesium

Take a dose of magnesium glycinate. It usually works within half an hour.

Can't sleep

What's happening

Fasting raises adrenaline, which can keep you awake even when you feel tired.

What to do

Magnesium in the evening

Take it an hour before bed to help you wind down.

Dark and cool

Sleep in a cool room, and put your phone and TV away two hours before bed.

Calming tea

Have a cup of lemon balm or chamomile tea.

Nausea

What's happening

It may come from bile collecting in your stomach, or from taking vitamins on an empty stomach.

What to do

Ginger

Ginger tea can settle your stomach.

Skip the pills for now

Vitamins often irritate an empty stomach. Leave them out until the nausea passes.

Heartburn

What's happening

There's no food to digest, so stomach acid can irritate your esophagus.

What to do

Apple cider vinegar

A spoonful of apple cider vinegar in water may help the valve at the top of your stomach close properly.

Stay upright

Drink sitting or standing, and don't lie down right after.

Diarrhea

What's happening

Usually it's from drinking salt or magnesium water too fast or too strong.

What to do

Sip slowly

Spread your mineral water over 30-60 minutes instead of drinking it all at once.

Less magnesium

Halve your magnesium dose until your stomach settles.

Irritable or on edge

What's happening

Your brain misses the pleasure of eating, so you may be more tense than usual.

What to do

Breathe

A few minutes of slow, deep breathing helps calm you down.

Walk

Take a 30-minute walk outside, ideally somewhere quiet.

It's the transition

The tension comes from the switch your body is making. It isn't your real mood.

Bored

What's happening

Eating fills a lot of the day. When it's gone, your brain looks for something to do and often reads that as hunger.

What to do

Have a plan

Reading, tidying, gardening: anything that keeps your hands and mind busy.

Hard tasks

Many people think clearly in the middle of a fast. Use that time for complicated work.

Brain fog

What's happening

When salt is low, nerve signals travel more slowly, and your thinking can slow down with them.

What to do

Salt

A little salt on your tongue can help clear your head.

Cold water

Splash cold water on your face. It wakes up your circulation and your attention.
